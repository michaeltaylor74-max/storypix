Turn a year of photos into the video everyone remembers.

StoryPix is a free Windows app for end-of-year showcases, graduations and farewells. Point it at your folders, add a song, and it builds the whole show. Then make it yours, slide by slide.

Download for Windows Read the guide

Free and open source. Windows 10 and 11, plus a Mac beta. Works offline, and your photos never leave your computer.

From folder to finished video

  1. 1

    Choose a folder

    Sub-folders like Year 7, Year 8 or Sports Day become sections. Put them in any order and name their title slides.

  2. 2

    Add your music

    Pick one song or several. StoryPix fits the show to the music, or to a length you choose.

  3. 3

    Build the show

    It sorts photos by date, skips burst duplicates, makes collages and adds transitions, and tells you if the pace is too fast.

  4. 4

    Make it yours

    Swap photos, change layouts, add captions, trim videos, zoom in on a face. Everything stays editable.

  5. 5

    Export

    Save a standard MP4 in HD or 4K that plays on any projector, TV, laptop or YouTube.

Built for the night of the showcase

Up to nine photos on a slide

When there are hundreds of photos to get through, collages keep the pace up without rushing. Choose from 25 layouts, or drag, resize and tilt photos wherever you like. White frames, rounded corners and polaroid styles are one click away.

Auto Build does the first draft

Tell it how long the show should be and it works out the rest. If 600 photos won't fit in five minutes, it says so plainly and offers choices: use a spread-out selection, more photos per slide, or a longer show.

Zoom in on the moment that matters

Draw a start frame and an end frame, and the camera glides from the whole photo to a face or a trophy. Gentle pan and zoom is added automatically everywhere else.

  • Videos fit right in. Trim clips to the best part, show several at once, and let the music dip automatically while someone speaks.
  • Music that behaves. Skip a song's intro, start it a few seconds in, crossfade between tracks.
  • Captions and titles with fonts, outlines and a gentle fade or rise.
  • Never lose work. Autosave every few seconds, recovery after a crash, and undo for everything.
  • Photos stay in proportion. Nothing is ever stretched or squashed unless you choose to.
  • Moves between computers. Open a show at home that you started at school, and StoryPix finds the photos.

Made by a teacher, for teachers

StoryPix started because the slideshow software we'd paid for kept crashing the week of the showcase, and every update came with a request to upgrade. This one is free, has no account, no watermark and no upsell, and its source code is open for anyone to check.

Download for Mac (beta)

Open the file and drag StoryPix into Applications. The first time, macOS says it can't check the app: click Done, then go to System Settings → Privacy & Security (older Macs: System Preferences → Security & Privacy), scroll down and click Open Anyway. The button shows for about an hour after macOS blocks the app.

Seeing a warning on Windows?

StoryPix is new and not code-signed yet, so Windows is cautious about it. That's normal for new free software.

  1. In Edge, if it says the file isn't commonly downloaded, hover over it and choose ⋯ → Keep → Show more → Keep anyway.
  2. If a blue Windows protected your PC box appears, choose More info → Run anyway.

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. No trial, no watermark, no account and no in-app purchases. It's open source under the MIT licence.

Does it upload our photos anywhere?

No. StoryPix works completely offline and never sends photos or anything else over the internet. Small preview copies are kept on your computer and deleted automatically after 30 days unused, or straight away from Help → Clear preview copies.

What files can I use?

Photos in JPG, PNG, HEIC, WebP and TIFF. Videos in MP4, MOV, AVI, MKV and most phone formats. Music in MP3, M4A, WAV, FLAC and more.

How long does it take to make the video?

Building the show takes seconds. Exporting a five-minute show in HD usually takes a few minutes on a typical school laptop.

Can I use it on a Mac or Chromebook?

There's a Mac beta for macOS 11 or later. It isn't signed by Apple yet, so the first time you open it you'll need to click Open Anyway in System Settings → Privacy & Security. There's no Chromebook version.

Something went wrong or I have an idea

Please open an issue on GitHub. Feedback from people who use it is the most useful thing there is.
